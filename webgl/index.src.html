Kerr

Each pixel traces a Kerr null geodesic backwards from the camera.

What you are seeing

  • Gravitational lensing. The far side of the disk is bent up over and under the shadow.
  • Doppler beaming. Gas orbiting towards you is blueshifted and brightened (≈ g⁴), gas receding is reddened and dimmed. The slider morphs from Nolan's symmetric film look (0) to the physical result (1).
  • Photon ring. Thin rings hugging the shadow are light that circled the hole once, twice…
  • D-shaped shadow. At high spin, prograde photons orbit closer: the shadow flattens on the approaching side. Toggle the analytic Bardeen curve to compare.
  • Frame dragging. The inner disk and the plunging gas inside the ISCO are swept round with the spin.

Try this

  • Slide Doppler & beaming from 0 (film look) to 1 (physical).
  • Hover the image: each pixel reports where its light came from — emission radius, redshift g, image order — or how far starlight was bent.
  • Switch View → Rays to see the lensing orders, or press C to overlay the analytic shadow.
  • Drag Orbital time (Accretion disk) to let the gas orbit: the inner disk overtakes the outer disk.

How it works

Null geodesics of the Kerr metric are integrated with the Boyer–Lindquist Hamiltonian (E = 1, L and Carter's Q conserved) in Mino time, with the angular motion written as a unit vector and an angular-momentum vector so the poles are regular. RK4 with an adaptive step (∝ distance to the horizon, refined near the disk). The camera is a zero-angular-momentum observer, so its local frame is exact. Along each ray the disk is integrated as a volume — emission and absorption — and the invariance of Iν/ν³ means the observed spectrum is a blackbody at g·T, converted to colour with the CIE 1931 observer. Gas follows circular Keplerian orbits Ω = 1/(r3/2+a) outside the ISCO and plunging geodesics inside it; its turbulence lives in differentially sheared co-rotating coordinates. Stars are analytic points filtered by the lensing Jacobian, so their magnification is automatic.

Artistic choices: visible-range disk temperatures (real disks peak in X-rays), turbulence, dust lanes, photon-ring emphasis. Geodesics, shadow, Doppler and gravitational shifts and beaming are physical.
